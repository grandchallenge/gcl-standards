## 1. Purpose and boundaries

This convention provides a reusable acknowledgment of the intellectual and creative labor of human contributors whose cumulative work has helped make contemporary artificial intelligence possible. It is an institutional acknowledgment of inheritance, not a claim of reconstructing the training corpus or influences of any particular model or generated passage.

**Two complementary practices are provided:**

1. **Human intellectual heritage acknowledgment.** A stable statement of collective intellectual debt, in long or short form.
2. **Work-specific AI participation and provenance disclosure.** A separate statement of actual AI use, verified sources, checks performed, and accountable authorship; see [the companion disclosure template](../templates/AI_PARTICIPATION_DISCLOSURE.md).

Neither statement replaces ordinary citation, quotation attribution, acknowledgement of identifiable contributors, copyright and licensing obligations, permissions, or independent technical verification. Authors must not imply that any particular individual or work contributed to a particular model's training unless supported by traceable evidence. The availability of a work for training is not proof of permission or consent.

## 2. Full acknowledgment

### Acknowledgment of Human Intellectual Heritage and AI Contributions

No work of knowledge stands alone.

The ideas, language, methods, and discoveries expressed in this work belong to a continuing human endeavour, built across generations through inquiry, imagination, experimentation, scholarship, and creative expression.

Where artificial intelligence has contributed to this work, we recognize that its capabilities have been made possible not only by advances in mathematics, science, and engineering, but also by the accumulated intellectual and creative labour of humanity. The writings, research, artworks, software, records, and other expressions of countless individuals and communities have helped form the foundations upon which contemporary AI systems are developed and trained.

We acknowledge the authors, scientists, philosophers, mathematicians, artists, educators, engineers, translators, archivists, and innumerable other contributors, known and unknown, whose works have enriched this collective inheritance. Many cannot be individually identified within the provenance of a particular AI-generated contribution; their absence from explicit attribution should not be mistaken for an absence of intellectual debt.

We further recognize that the development and use of artificial intelligence raise legitimate questions concerning attribution, consent, compensation, ownership, and stewardship of human creative works. This acknowledgment does not presume those questions to be settled, nor does it replace specific citations, established scholarly obligations, or the rights of individual creators.

We regard artificial intelligence neither as an origin without antecedents nor as a substitute for the human intellect, but as a new instrument through which humanity may engage with, recombine, challenge, and extend its accumulated knowledge.

**In contributing to the continuing edifice of knowledge, we acknowledge those who laid its foundations, those whose work sustains it, and those who will build upon what we leave behind.**

### Disclosure of AI Participation

Artificial intelligence contributed to the preparation of this work as disclosed in the accompanying authorship or methodology statement. The identified accountable authors or publishers remain responsible for its substantive claims, evidentiary standards, interpretation, and publication, subject to applicable law and publication policies.

## 3. Short acknowledgment

### Acknowledgment of Human Intellectual Heritage

This work acknowledges its debt to the accumulated knowledge, creativity, and intellectual labour of humanity. Where artificial intelligence has assisted in its development, we recognize that its capabilities are founded upon generations of human discovery and expression, including the contributions of countless authors, scholars, artists, scientists, and others whose individual works may not be identifiable in a particular output.

We recognize these contributions as part of our shared intellectual inheritance. This acknowledgment does not replace specific attribution, citation, or the rights of creators, nor does it presume consent to the use of their works in AI training.

In building upon this inheritance, we seek to contribute responsibly to the continuing edifice of human knowledge.

## 4. Usage and editorial interpretation

- **Long form:** Prefer for substantial publications or institutional statements; pair with a factual, work-specific AI participation disclosure.
- **Short form:** Prefer for publication footers, articles, documentation, and software repositories, subject to editorial constraints; pair with the same factual disclosure when AI materially contributed.
- **No material AI participation:** Do not imply there was AI assistance where none is known to have occurred. An institution may acknowledge broader intellectual heritage independently, but must not add a false AI-participation assertion.
- **Specific citations:** Retain all actual named scholarly and creative influences; the general acknowledgment cannot satisfy source-specific attribution.
- **Unknown training sources:** State that model-level training provenance is not independently known where that is the case; do not ascribe works, individuals, jurisdictions, rights, or licenses to a model based on inference.
- **Higher-priority rules:** Honor journal, publisher, collaborator, formal-library, competition, funder, repository, and legal requirements, even when stricter than this template.
- **No rights inference:** Nothing here asserts creator consent, legally authorized model training, compensation, fair use/fair dealing, or freedom from infringement.
- **Responsibility:** Identify the accountable author or publisher and preserve distinct verification and provenance for any substantive claims.

## 5. Council review and maintenance questions

1. Does the acknowledgment recognize collective human work without appropriating or obscuring identifiable authorship?
2. Are claims about training-data provenance appropriately bounded?
3. Are consent, rights, compensation, and licensing explicitly left open rather than presumed?
4. Do the long and short forms preserve consistent meaning and usable diction?
5. Can the companion disclosure be completed truthfully for opaque proprietary and open systems alike?
6. Are publication and adoption gates separate from standards admission and Council recommendation?

For a material successor revision, Council shall record exact-subject findings from the appropriate offices under effective governance; changed source bytes invalidate stale review evidence.

## 6. Admission, adoption, and release

Version 0.1.0 was **Council accepted and admitted** through its [protected admission record](../admissions/GCL-HIH-00-0.1.0.json). This 0.1.1 editorial successor has no effect merely because its source is visible; its effective status is governed by the [current registry status](../status/GCL-HIH-00-current.json) and a separate exact-revision admission. Issue creation, CI success, or PR existence never confer programme adoption or public-release authority.

Council disposition and protected exact-revision admission establish standards-layer status. Individual GCL programmes adopt admitted versions separately where programme-wide conformance is sought. Formal publication as an endorsed external institutional statement requires the Human Steward's separate exact-publication authorization and release readback. No successor becomes effective solely through this repository's custody.

**Revision triggers:** credible provenance objections, legal/publisher incompatibility, inability to make a truthful participation disclosure, material ambiguities regarding human rights or responsibility, or an adverse Council finding.
