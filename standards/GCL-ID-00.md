# GCL-ID-00: Identifiability Preflight and Symmetry-Kernel Doctrine

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Candidate normative standard; no cross-programme effect until protected admission and programme adoption  
**Registry:** `grandchallenge/gcl-standards`  
**Council issue:** `grandchallenge/gcl-standards#88`  
**Scope:** Inverse, reconstruction, calibration, latent-recovery, system-identification, and parameter-recovery work

## 1. Purpose

GCL work SHALL distinguish information that is absent from the declared observations from information that is merely difficult to recover numerically.

Before substantial investment in an inverse solver, estimator, learned reconstructor, optimizer, or search procedure, an in-scope work package SHALL characterize the observation-preserving equivalence relation on its proposed recovery target.

When that equivalence is generated by a symmetry action, the work SHOULD compute or bound the corresponding symmetry kernel, orbit directions, stabilizer, or quotient dimension before solver optimization.

The compact operational rule is:

> **Before solving an inverse problem, characterize what the observation map has already transformed away.**

The phrase "symmetry kernel" is shorthand. This standard does not assume that every problem is linear, differentiable, continuous, or described by a group action.

## 2. Governing principle

Let (X) denote the admissible latent or parameter space, (Y) the declared observation space, and

[
F:X	o Y
]

the observation map, forward model, evidence relation, or experimentally realized measurement process.

Define observational equivalence by

[
xsim_F x'
quadLongleftrightarrowquad
F(x)=F(x')
]

or by the declared equality/tolerance relation appropriate to the evidence model.

The object recoverable from the observations is, in general, not (x) itself but its equivalence class

[
[x]_Fin X/{sim_F}.
]

A stronger claim that a particular representative (x) is recoverable requires additional information sufficient to select that representative.

No solver can infer a distinction that the declared observation relation erases unless new information, prior structure, normalization, intervention, or measurement is introduced.

## 3. Scope

This standard applies when a work package proposes to infer hidden structure from observations, including:

- inverse problems and parameter reconstruction;
- latent-state or representation recovery;
- system identification;
- geometric reconstruction;
- calibration and boundary-factor recovery;
- inverse rendering or inverse graphics;
- tomography and sensing;
- learned inverse maps;
- inverse PDE or operator problems;
- gauge- or coordinate-dependent parameter recovery;
- model extraction from sufficient statistics or compressed observables;
- scientific inference where several admissible states may induce the same measured data.

Routine direct computation with a declared one-to-one map is exempt unless a material non-identifiability question arises.

## 4. Required identifiability preflight

Before a work package treats solver quality as the primary research frontier, it SHALL record the following.

### ID-1: Recovery target

State exactly what is to be recovered.

The target SHALL distinguish physical, geometric, semantic, gauge-dependent, coordinate-dependent, and implementation-dependent quantities where those distinctions are material.

A phrase such as "recover the parameters" is insufficient when multiple parameterizations represent the same observable object.

### ID-2: Observation contract

State what information the solver actually receives.

The contract SHALL identify, as applicable:

- the forward map or measurement relation;
- observed variables and omitted variables;
- boundary conditions and interventions;
- tolerances, noise model, quantization, or finite precision;
- known priors or constraints supplied to the solver;
- any side information not contained in the primary observation.

Information used during training, calibration, branch construction, or fixture generation SHALL NOT silently count as information available to the inverse procedure.

### ID-3: Observation-preserving equivalence

Characterize known transformations or distinct admissible states that preserve the declared observations.

The work SHALL test for relevant:

- gauge transformations;
- coordinate changes;
- relabelings and permutations;
- scaling symmetries;
- sign, phase, rotation, translation, or basis ambiguities;
- latent reparameterizations;
- boundary rescalings;
- discrete branch symmetries;
- continuous null directions;
- other observational equivalences specific to the model.

Absence of a known symmetry SHALL NOT be reported as proof of injectivity.

### ID-4: Kernel or orbit analysis

Where a differentiable local model exists, the work SHOULD inspect the differential (DF_x) and identify null directions or rank deficiency.

Where a group action (Gcurvearrowright X) is known to preserve observations, the work SHOULD characterize the induced orbit and stabilizer.

Where neither representation is appropriate, the work SHALL use the strongest available exact, algebraic, combinatorial, probabilistic, or constructive equivalence argument.

The objective is to characterize the non-identifiable class, not to force every problem into linear-algebra language.

### ID-5: Recoverable quotient

State what the observations do identify.

The result SHOULD take one of these forms where possible:

- an injective target;
- a quotient (X/{sim_F});
- a set of invariants separating observational equivalence classes;
- a locally identifiable subspace;
- an identifiable rank or dimension;
- an interval/set-valued identified region;
- an explicit unresolved equivalence class.

A work package SHALL prefer a precise quotient-identifiable result over an unsupported full-recovery claim.

### ID-6: Symmetry-breaking information

If full representative recovery is required, state what extra information removes the ambiguity.

Examples include:

- gauge fixing;
- canonical normalization;
- additional boundary data;
- new measurements;
- interventions;
- anchor points;
- labels;
- temporal observations;
- source-defined compatibility conditions;
- independently justified priors.

The work SHALL distinguish a genuine new observation from a convention that merely chooses one representative.

### ID-7: Minimality test

Where practical, test whether the proposed added information is sufficient and whether fewer independent conditions could suffice.

If an ambiguity has dimension or rank (k), a proposal to remove it SHOULD account for how its added information constrains those (k) directions.

The standard does not require a universal minimality proof. It requires that the amount and role of symmetry-breaking information be made explicit.

### ID-8: Solver-worthiness gate

A solver campaign MAY proceed against the full target only after the work has either:

1. established identifiability for that target under the declared observation contract; or
2. declared the additional normalization, prior, or measurement that selects a representative.

Otherwise the solver SHALL be evaluated against the recoverable quotient, invariant, set, or partial target.

A structural ambiguity SHALL NOT be treated as an optimization defect.

### ID-9: Failure classification

Observed reconstruction error SHALL, where evidence permits, be classified separately as:

- `STRUCTURAL_NON_IDENTIFIABILITY`;
- `QUOTIENT_IDENTIFIABLE_ONLY`;
- `IDENTIFIABLE_WITH_DECLARED_NORMALIZATION`;
- `LOCAL_OR_PARTIAL_IDENTIFIABILITY`;
- `NUMERICAL_ILL_CONDITIONING`;
- `FINITE_DATA_OR_NOISE_LIMITED`;
- `MODEL_MISMATCH`;
- `OPTIMIZATION_OR_SEARCH_FAILURE`;
- `UNRESOLVED`.

These classes may coexist. A work package SHALL NOT collapse structural non-identifiability into optimization failure merely because an optimizer cannot select the intended representative.

### ID-10: Claim boundary

An identifiability preflight is a statement about the declared model and observations.

It SHALL NOT, by itself, establish:

- correctness of the forward model;
- physical truth of a latent representation;
- statistical consistency outside the declared assumptions;
- numerical stability;
- optimality of a chosen estimator;
- mathematical certification;
- product, safety, deployment, manufacturing, patent, or commercial claims.

For mathematics campaigns, MATHCERT retains sole certification authority through admitted routes.

## 5. Required negative test

Every in-scope work package SHOULD attempt at least one constructive ambiguity test:

1. produce two distinct admissible latent states with equal declared observations; or
2. show why the known candidate symmetry action is trivial on the target; or
3. establish injectivity by an appropriate theorem or certified finite argument.

A constructive indistinguishable pair is decisive evidence against full identifiability under the declared observation contract.

## 6. Structural ambiguity versus poor conditioning

Identifiability and conditioning are different questions.

An injective map may be badly conditioned. A non-injective map may be numerically stable on its quotient.

Accordingly:

[
	ext{non-identifiable}
otequiv	ext{ill-conditioned}
]

and

[
	ext{ill-conditioned}
otequiv	ext{non-identifiable}.
]

Solver benchmarks SHALL NOT substitute for this distinction.

## 7. Learned inverse models

Training data can hide an identifiability defect by consistently selecting one representative from each observational equivalence class.

A learned inverse model that predicts that representative has learned a selection rule induced by its data or prior. It has not demonstrated that the observation uniquely determines the representative.

In-scope learned systems SHALL distinguish:

- information present in the observation;
- information supplied by the training distribution or prior;
- information supplied by architecture or regularization;
- information introduced by a canonicalization rule.

This distinction is especially important when evaluating apparent "recovery" of gauge-dependent latent variables.

## 8. Relation to representation change

When the scientific object is invariant under a representation change, the primary recovery target SHOULD be formulated on the invariant quotient unless there is an independent reason to recover a particular representation.

This standard therefore treats representational ambiguity as first-class structure rather than nuisance error.

The practical question is:

[
oxed{	ext{What structure survives the observation map, and what has been transformed away?}}
]

## 9. Motivating protected example: VGSE-ENG-WP06

VGSE-ENG-WP06 provides the motivating certified example for this doctrine.

The Programme result distinguishes two categories:

1. for a source-defined TE3 t-embedding, Euclidean geometry determines the graph-weight gauge class and all eight quotient directions;
2. for the broader algebraic realization class without TE3, an exact positive boundary-rescaling symmetry preserves primitive geometry while acting with rank five on the five boundary-path quotient coordinates.

Thus primitive geometry identifies three quotient directions and leaves an exact five-dimensional ambiguity in the broader class.

The missing five directions are not geometric observables waiting for a better inverse solver. They require symmetry-breaking information equivalent in role to the source's boundary-fixed TE3 compatibility condition.

Protected evidence:

- MATH-PROGRAMME substantive result: `9663a9bc8f71af808ea4578a02467cfd47c29d92`;
- MATH-PROGRAMME terminal checkpoint: `f132000f8308c65ac89c31d90d5e22df6c5dc56c`;
- MATHCERT TE3 conformance audit: `15b68c196d020045bea42fc34236e0647b87cbb9`;
- MATHCERT C05 current-state reconciliation: `ff8f2509d8d0603a2712abcc43c1d450136ee4b3`.

This example motivates the doctrine. It does not prove that every inverse problem possesses a five-dimensional ambiguity, a gauge group, or any particular algebraic form.

## 10. Recommended work-product

An adopted programme SHOULD leave one concise identifiability preflight record containing:

- target;
- observation contract;
- known equivalence/symmetry;
- evidence for invariance;
- identifiable quotient/invariants/rank;
- required symmetry-breaking information;
- selected failure classification;
- solver target after preflight;
- unresolved assumptions;
- claim boundary;
- exact supporting evidence identities.

The registry provides `templates/identifiability_preflight.yaml` as a non-authoritative starting point.

## 11. Stopping rule

A work package has reached a legitimate identifiability stopping boundary when it has constructed or certified a nontrivial observational equivalence that the declared observations cannot break.

At that point, further attempts to recover the separated representatives from the same information SHOULD stop.

The next research question must be one of:

- recover only the quotient or invariants;
- add justified information;
- impose and disclose a normalization;
- change the target;
- challenge the ambiguity proof.

"Try a stronger solver" is not, by itself, a response to a proved structural ambiguity.

## 12. Conformance statement

A work package conforms to GCL-ID-00 only when its inverse/reconstruction target and observation contract are explicit, relevant observational equivalences have been investigated, the recoverable object is stated at the strongest supported level, structural ambiguity is separated from numerical/optimization failure, and any representative-selection mechanism is disclosed.

Documentation that merely repeats the slogan "compute the symmetry kernel" without an observation contract and recoverability analysis is not conformance.

## 13. Authority boundary

GCL-ID-00 is a methodological and evidentiary standard.

It does not determine mathematical truth, certify a theorem, alter programme authority, or create organization-wide effect merely by being present in the registry.

Candidate text becomes normative only through the applicable protected admission and programme-adoption processes.
